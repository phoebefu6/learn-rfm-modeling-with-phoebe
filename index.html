Learn RFM Modeling with Phoebe

Every customer can be described with three numbers: how long since they last bought, how often they have bought, how much they have spent. That is RFM, the oldest segmentation still in daily use, and it is cheap, honest and needs nothing but an order table. It is also a chain of small decisions that most tutorials make silently, and the biggest of them is where the knife goes between high and low. On 93,358 real marketplace customers, of whom 97 percent bought exactly once, the share called "best" runs from 1 percent to 16 percent depending on nothing but that knife, and under two popular vendor defaults nine in ten of the "best" bought once. Six sessions on RFM as a decision system: the cut, the dimensions, the action, the proof, and keeping it alive.

The six sessions 🎯 · from raw orders to a model somebody can defend

The running data is the public Olist marketplace dataset: 99,441 real orders, recomputed here into one row per customer. Four more cases join it where the purchase table alone runs out: Taobao browsing behaviour, Douyin live rooms, Pinduoduo team purchase, and a small WeChat community shop.

🧾 Session 1 · core

Three numbers and a snapshot date

R, F and M defined precisely, the six decisions hiding inside them, the clock that has to be frozen before any of the three exists, and the eight-group taxonomy read as a mechanism. Build-along on 93,358 real customers.

▶ Start here 🔪 Session 2 · core

The cut decides the segment

Mean, median, thirds, fifths, fifths-then-collapse and merchant thresholds on the same skewed pile. Why fifths on a column that is 97 percent ties are decided by row order, and what a tiny community shop does to every one of them.

same people, five knives 🎛️ Session 3 · bench night

The RFM bench

4,000 real customers, seven cuts, every segment share and revenue share counted in your browser. Two vendor defaults call 13 to 16 percent of them Champions; nine in ten of those bought once. Plus the one-total-score trap.

★ the bench 📡 Session 4 · core

Beyond purchase

When 98 percent of the rows are not purchases. Engagement in place of spend on Taobao, live-room loyalty as a frequency on Douyin, and the referral axis Pinduoduo's team purchase adds. Which axis replaces M and which one adds.

RFE, rooms, referrals 📨 Session 5 · core

From segment to action, and proving it

The action table per group, the tool defaults that quietly decide it for you, and the holdout that tells a lift from a coincidence. Why "message everyone in win-back" is the most expensive sentence in RFM.

the holdout gate 🔁 Session 6 · core

Keep it alive

Recompute on a cadence, watch groups drift between snapshots, write the one-page RFM spec, and know when the model has done its job and should hand off to lifetime value or to clustering. Final scorecard.
